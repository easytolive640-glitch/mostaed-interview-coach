A 30-day career plan you can actually follow

Replace scattered applications with a weekly rhythm of research, evidence, practice and follow-up.

By Mostaed · Practical guidance, not a guarantee of employment

Week 1: choose one direction to test

Choose one target role and read several current job descriptions. Note repeated skills, location requirements and experience levels. Separate skills you can already demonstrate from gaps you need to work on. Set a realistic weekly schedule around your responsibilities. This is an experiment to focus your search, not a permanent career decision.

Week 2: prepare evidence of your skills

Improve the CV sections most relevant to your target role. Prepare one small work sample or a clear example from real experience. For IT, this could be a documented troubleshooting lab; for customer service, a fictional complaint-resolution scenario labelled as practice. Explain your reasoning and limitations. Do not present a practice exercise as paid client work.

Week 3: apply deliberately and practise

Apply to roles that match your evidence and tailor the opening CV section to each opportunity. Keep a simple tracker with the role, date, link and next action. Practise explaining your most relevant examples. When contacting a professional, make your request specific and respectful. Avoid mass messages that imply a personal connection you do not have.

Week 4: review what you can control

Review the relevance of your applications, the clarity of your CV and your interview answers. Track actions completed as well as responses received; hiring outcomes depend on factors beyond your control. Choose one change for the next month. Your next action: write down your target role, three weekly tasks and a date to review your progress.

Put the advice into practice

Choose your interview category and rehearse five questions for free. Use real details from your experience.
