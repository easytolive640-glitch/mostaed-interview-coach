Prepare for an interview without memorising a script

Build a small bank of real examples and practise answering the question in front of you.

By Mostaed · Practical guidance, not a guarantee of employment

Identify what the interviewer needs to learn

Read the job description and choose three important responsibilities. For each, identify an experience that shows how you work. Prepare an introduction that connects your relevant background with this role. Avoid listing your entire CV. You can draw on study, volunteering or personal projects if you explain that context honestly.

Use STAR when the question asks for an example

For a past experience, outline the situation, task, action and result. Spend most of the answer on your actions. A motivation question needs a clear reason and relevant evidence, not a forced story. A technical question needs an accurate explanation or a troubleshooting approach. Let the question determine the structure.

Rehearse flexibility, not identical wording

Answer one question aloud in about a minute, then repeat it using different wording. Ask yourself what an interviewer might challenge: your specific role, the evidence, or a trade-off. If you lose your place, pause and return to the main point. Do not invent an answer to hide a gap; explain what you know and how you would check the rest.

Finish with a useful review

Before the interview, check the meeting link, time zone and a quiet place to talk. Prepare two questions about expectations, team work or success in the role. After practice, choose one weakness to improve rather than rewriting every answer. Your next action: rehearse three real examples and one follow-up question for each.

Put the advice into practice

Choose your interview category and rehearse five questions for free. Use real details from your experience.
