Turn CV responsibilities into credible achievements

Make your contribution easier to understand without inventing metrics or overstating your role.

By Mostaed · Practical guidance, not a guarantee of employment

Start with the role you want

Read one relevant job description and identify three skills you can demonstrate. For customer service, these might be complaint handling, accurate follow-up and prioritisation. For IT, they might be troubleshooting, security and incident communication. Select experiences that show those skills. Keep your job title and employment dates accurate.

Write action, scope and evidence

Replace a broad task such as “handled customer complaints” with what you actually did: “Tracked unresolved complaints, coordinated with the delivery team and followed up with customers until resolution.” Add a verified result if you have one. If you do not have a reliable number, describe an observable outcome rather than estimating a percentage.

Keep team results and your contribution separate

If a project succeeded through a team effort, explain which part you owned. Use “contributed to” when you supported the work and “led” only when you led it. Before adding a metric, check the time period, starting point and source. Avoid confidential customer names, internal documents or data you are not permitted to share.

Practise explaining one CV bullet

Choose one bullet and explain the situation, your responsibility, your actions and the outcome aloud. Then answer: “What would you do differently?” If you cannot explain a claim clearly, revise it before applying. Your next action: improve three CV bullets and prepare one honest interview example for each.

Put the advice into practice

Choose your interview category and rehearse five questions for free. Use real details from your experience.
