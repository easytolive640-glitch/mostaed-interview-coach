Customer service interview questions and sample answers

Practise customer service interview questions on complaints, escalation and prioritisation, with fictional examples and a free practice option.

By Mostaed · Sample answers are fictional illustrations, not claims to copy.

Show how you help customers

Customer service answers should demonstrate listening, clear communication, responsible escalation and follow-through. Prepare examples involving an unhappy customer, several urgent requests and an error you corrected. If you are new to the field, use relevant experience from retail, volunteering or a group project.

How would you handle an angry customer?

Explain how you would listen without interruption, acknowledge the problem, confirm the facts and offer an option within policy. Do not promise a refund or deadline you cannot authorise. Fictional example: “A customer contacted us about a late parcel. I checked the tracking information, explained the available options and agreed when I would update them. I followed up at that time, even though the delivery was still pending.” This demonstrates ownership without inventing a happy outcome.

Tell me about a problem you resolved

Use STAR: situation, task, action and result. Name your specific action rather than saying “we fixed it.” Fictional example: “Several customers could not find the returns form. I reproduced the problem, shared the broken link with our website team and provided the correct link in my replies. After the link was corrected, I checked that the form worked.” Add a number only if you actually measured it.

How do you prioritise customer requests?

Describe the criteria you use: severity, urgency, customer impact, service commitments and dependencies. Explain how you keep lower-priority customers informed. A good answer recognises that the loudest complaint is not always the most urgent. If several cases have equal priority, describe the queue rules or ask a supervisor for direction.

When should you escalate?

Escalate when a case exceeds your authority, requires specialist access or involves a risk you cannot resolve. Pass on a concise summary, steps already taken and the customer’s expectations. Keep ownership of communication where your process allows it. Never share real customer names or confidential account information in a practice answer.

A short rehearsal checklist

Choose one example and answer in about a minute. Did you explain the customer’s problem? Did you describe what you personally did? Is the result accurate?
